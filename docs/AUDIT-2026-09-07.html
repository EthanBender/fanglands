The short version

  • Every item from Colin's three design conversations exists in the game. The main story runs from the cave to The Fang across 17 quest stages, and the self-test plays all of it.
  • Five defects can trap a player permanently (a respawn point inside a wall, a home teleport into solid rock, climbing out of a machine into a tree, a ferryman who never comes back, and a save reload that erases Death's chest). All five have one-line fixes.
  • The economy has three farms (the Fang and the Barrelbeast respawn with their legendary loot, and Dunstan buys potatoes for three times Greta's price) and the anvil panel hides 12 of its 26 recipes on any screen shorter than 1,140 px, including every Godly Plated piece.
  • Balance is right for the first hour and wrong after it. Chapter 3's walker and the two dragons the main quest sends you to are out of reach at the levels the story hands you, and the two features Colin named as "what makes it different" sit behind the two biggest grind walls.
  • Nothing on screen tells a new player where to go: the main quest is untracked by default, and the world map's quest marker stops updating after Chapter 3.

Gathering itself is well paced: 1.5 to 4 seconds per log or ore depending on the tool, which is 20,000 to 60,000 XP an hour and makes the low gates quick. The problem is only the three headline gates, which sit far past anything the story funds.

Recommended numbers

  • Give the Duke's Chapter 3 task a middle step: a bulldozer or brute bounty at Melee 8, and have him send you to Ironclad Isle for the iron battleaxe and steel bars before the walker.
  • Add a level-30 wingless "ash drake" near Dunstan's farm that drops dung every time, so the main quest does not require a level-45 kill; keep green and red dragons as optional loot.
  • Godly Plated at Smithing 32 / 34 / 36 / 40, with a dragon-scale smelting recipe as the 30-to-40 filler. Hover armour at Crafting 12, with the Queen's quest paying 300 Crafting XP.
  • The Fang: 800 HP and defence 50, or keep the stats and make the four elements teach dodging so the fight is skill, not food. No respawn after the kill; the Barrelbeast likewise.
  • A kill bonus of 10 XP per monster level for anything level 10 and up, so bosses pay in progress as well as loot.
  • Companions scale with the knight's combat level; at a fixed level 12 they do under 1 damage a second to a dragon.

3 · Player experience

Where a ten-year-old gets lost

The first twenty minutes are well guided: the Voice names each step from the sword to the Duke, and the "not yet" lines show live numbers. After Thistledown the guidance falls away.

  • The main quest is invisible until tracked. Nothing is on screen by default and nothing says "press Track". Auto-track the main quest at new game and on every chapter change.
  • The world map lies after Chapter 3. The gold marker knows stages 0 to 7 and then points at the Duke forever. Features need a way to register a map target for their stages.
  • Deepholm has no surface hint. One villager mentions coal in the quarry; nobody mentions a mine shaft. A line from Brakka and a Voice line near the shaft would do it.
  • Nobody sends you to the jungle. The totem hint is good once you are there, but no surface NPC mentions elves, the loom or the hover armour. The Hollowford survivors are the natural place.
  • The notice board is never mentioned. Its marker only appears when a job is ready. The cave-road board deserves a Voice line.
  • The Fang's gate says "sealed" and nothing else. A salved player can reach it before stage 14; the line should point at Dunstan.
  • Heroes are found only by bumping into them. Sera's cage is behind the level-18 walker, so the first companion cannot help with the first hard fight; Garrick at the inn is unmentioned. Dorran should say a retired guard drinks in his inn.

Text and dialogue

  • Dialogue auto-advances too fast for a child. The Duke's 103-character training line is gone in 8 seconds; a ten-year-old reads about 10 characters a second. There is no dialogue log, and any tap in the bottom band skips a line on touch. Wait for a tap, grow the box for wrapped lines, add a "last said" tab to the quest panel, and pause monsters while the Voice speaks.
  • Notices and quest lines truncate on phones. The notice box holds about 51 characters on a 375 px phone; 25 notices exceed it, including the machine controls. The quest panel cuts lines at about 30 characters on phones.
  • Banners fight. A rare drop banner is overwritten by the chapter banner in the same tick; area banner, level banner, notice and two Voice lines can all show at once entering Hollowford.
  • Key names in speech. The Voice says "Swing with Space" and "Open your quests (J)"; Wren says "Space shoots"; twelve notices name keys. On touch the buttons are called SWING, USE and QUESTS. A key-name helper would fix all of them at once.
  • Tone. The Voice, Death, the Duke, Thrain, Aelith, Harl and Dunstan are consistent. Tobin's "Dude. Dude." is the one modern line, and it is Colin's own, so it stays.

Touch and small screens

  • On a 375 px phone the MENU, SKILLS and HELP buttons draw on top of the minimap, and the BAG button overlaps SKILLS.
  • Home (H) has no standing touch button; it only appears after selecting an item in the bag.
  • Panels close only via a 30 × 26 px "×"; the map panel promises "tap anywhere" and does not. Tapping the map swings the sword.
  • The bank shows 15 of 60 vault slots on a phone; the anvil shows 6 of 26 recipes in landscape.
  • Disabled buttons on desktop are not registered, so clicking a greyed recipe closes the whole panel.

Kid-friendliness

  • "New game (erases save)" is one unconfirmed tap. The most dangerous control in the game needs a second tap.
  • Drop and Sell have no confirmation, and things on the ground are not saved, so a refresh loses overflow loot.
  • A second death replaces Death's chest. The design doc promises coins are never lost; with coins as a pack item, two deaths in the camp lose the first purse. Death should keep every pack, or coins should never enter the chest.
  • Dying in a piloted machine loses it, and nothing says it comes back in four minutes.
  • The grind walls are boring rather than hard: mine, buy coal, smelt, smith, sell, repeat.

5 · Suggested next wave

A third wave of agents, in the order that unblocks the most. The first two are fixes and belong in the core, so they go first and alone; the rest are independent feature files.

  1. Core fix pass (one agent, core only): the seven soft-lock and loss defects, the three farms, coin loss on sell, the double fee, regrow overwriting, hover walk-over for the player only, a shared left-HUD cursor, saves by tile name, a talk-before hook so features can extend the Duke.
  2. Core UX pass (one agent, core only): auto-track and map targets, dialogue pacing and log, key-name helper, confirmations, panel paging and tabs, the phone layout collisions, ground drops saved.
  3. Rebalance (one agent, mostly data): the numbers in section 2 plus the ash drake and companion scaling.
  4. Hollowford rebuilt (feature file 30).
  5. Title screen, save slots and region music (feature files 31 and 32).

6 · Tech debt worth knowing

  • The in-browser suite now takes about 30 s and the headless runner about 60 s; both are fine but the browser tool's limit means the suite has to be started asynchronously. Splitting the self-test into core and per-feature runs would let a single feature's checks run in seconds.
  • Three feature files draw their own projectiles because the core renders every non-arrow projectile as a bomb; a projectile draw hook would remove that duplication.
  • Region ordering is held together by four files inserting at computed positions to keep one test's assumption that Deepholm is first. The test should assert on region lookups, not array order.
  • Two hot spots on the 200 × 140 map: pairwise monster separation over all 170 monsters every frame, and a full 28,000-cell minimap repaint whenever a new tile is first changed. Both have easy fixes.
  • Wandering villagers only wander inside Thistledown, so dwarves and elves stand still.
